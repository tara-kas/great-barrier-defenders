# Offline material assets

The texture bundle is under `assets/materials/`; coral specimen geometry and its source textures are bundled separately under `assets/corals/` and `assets/pocillopora/`. Renderers resolve paths relative to the repository and author absolute `Sdf.AssetPath` texture paths when building a USD stage. All maps and geometry are available offline; neither Poly Haven's API nor an asset-server connection is needed at runtime.

## Seabed

Use **`seabed/fine_sand_*` as the primary close-range seabed material**. This is the scanned Sand 03 material by Charlotte Baglioni from Poly Haven, with diffuse colour, roughness and OpenGL normal maps at 2048×2048. Its documented physical width is 2 m. At that repeat scale it gives fine gritty grains and restrained creases instead of the old uniformly coloured mesh.

`seabed/sand_*` contains the larger Aerial Beach 01 ripple material. Its native width is 30 m. It can be used on a broad background sand patch at native scale, or repeated at 6–8 m for a deliberately compressed visual ripple pattern. Any scale compression is an artistic setting, not the scan's real measurement.

`seabed/rubble_*` contains Coral Ground 02 by Rob Tuytel: porous limestone with small gravel and sandy debris. Native repeat is 2 m. This suits reef substrate, exposed dead skeleton bases and outcrops; it should not be applied as living coral tissue.

Diffuse maps are sRGB. Normal and roughness maps are non-colour/raw data. Roughness is the grayscale `r` channel. For `UsdPreviewSurface`, connect a `UsdUVTexture` normal sample with scale `[2,2,2,1]` and bias `[-1,-1,-1,0]` so the stored [0,1] RGB values become a signed tangent-space normal. The maps are **OpenGL (+Y)**, not DirectX. If reducing normal strength, scale X/Y and renormalize in the consuming shader rather than reversing green.

## Coral microdetail

`coral/coral_normal_gl.png`, `coral_roughness.png`, `coral_albedo_detail.png` and a 16-bit `coral_height.png` are 1024×1024 periodic maps generated by `scripts/build_material_assets.py`. The map contains jittered small calice cups with rounded rims and subtle radial septa, plus weak tissue grain. It is intentionally not coarse random rock noise.

Use a repeat width of roughly **0.08–0.12 m** on branching coral surfaces. There are 28 calices across the tile, so the apparent calice spacing is around 3–4 mm at that setting. Normal strength should remain restrained; these maps add surface detail while the organic mesh supplies the colony silhouette. They can be used with vertex colour to preserve species/health palettes by connecting normal and roughness only. The nearly white `coral_albedo_detail.png` may instead be multiplied by a constant colony tint using `UsdUVTexture.scale` if desired.

The source generator is offline and deterministic:

```bash
python3 scripts/build_material_assets.py
```

These are artistic surface approximations, not calibrated biological scans. Their UV field should be continuous across branch rings to avoid seams, with one UV seam at the back of each branch.

## UV-aligned Pocillopora tissue materials

`pocillopora_tissue/` contains 18 generated PBR maps derived from the
Smithsonian specimen's original 1024×1024 diffuse atlas: seven muted healthy
phenotypes, seven partly bleached living variants, one dead diffuse control
and three roughness maps. The original normal atlas in `assets/pocillopora/`
is reused. All source and derived map hashes, licence and conversion details
are in `pocillopora_tissue/manifest.json` and its `README.md`.

The original glTF occlusion atlas (`pocillopora_occlusion.png`) restores local calice-cup contrast in the diffuse maps, without baking broad occlusion shadows into the whole colony.

The conversion partly normalizes broad museum lighting while retaining fine
calice contrast. Bleached living variants retain warm ivory tissue,
pink-brown calice rims and residual pigment islands; they do not use dead
algal staining. Dead maps have dark brown/olive biofilm cues and higher
roughness. These are artistic state cues derived from a dried specimen,
not measured living tissue, fluorescence or a bleaching-severity estimator.

Use `reefsim.reef_tissue.material_files(health, phenotype=None)`. The default
tan diffuse phenotype is shared by the known R/G contact genets; emitted
fluorescence is a separate property. Diffuse maps are sRGB with unit texture
scale, roughness is raw grayscale and normal is raw OpenGL data. Bind the
diffuse map directly rather than multiplying it by a solid pigment tint.
The detailed renderer uses low specular reflectance and no clearcoat.

These maps must use the corresponding Smithsonian UV layout. Registered
living/bleached close-up colonies use the preserved 150,000-triangle hero
mesh, avoiding UV errors from nearest-vertex transfer during simplification.
Background scans retain the 19,908-triangle mesh. Other procedural geometry
uses muted `tissue_color()` values and its own microdetail UVs; applying the
museum atlas to unrelated branch UVs would be incorrect. Regenerate the maps
offline with `python3 assets/materials/pocillopora_tissue/generate.py`.

## Verification and provenance

The habitat also bundles a real Smithsonian CC0 *Acropora secale* scan,
with its museum plinth removed and scenario pigments applied. See
[SCANNED_CORALS.md](SCANNED_CORALS.md) for specimen context, conversion,
license and checksums. The Acropora scan supplies baseline colonies and selected background colonies.

The current contact and controlled inspection scenes use the Smithsonian
*Pocillopora damicornis*, USNM 46926, scan for living and bleached colony
morphology. The bundled CC0/public-domain source is a dried Galápagos specimen,
not one of the study genets or a Great Barrier Reef survey. The derived whole
colony retains its natural aspect ratio and source normal texture; scenario
health/pigment materials are applied separately. The detailed contact path
uses all 150,000 source triangles and original UV seams for hero colonies,
while reduced scans remain in the background. The recipient contact surface
is a selected triangle of the unchanged scan. Only the small basal securing
compound and manipulated cuttings are separate engineering geometry. See
[POCILLOPORA_SCAN.md](POCILLOPORA_SCAN.md) and
`assets/pocillopora/manifest.json` for source URLs, conversion and checksums.
All specimen assets are available offline.

The downloaded seabed JPEGs are 2048×2048; generated coral maps and the
derived Pocillopora tissue maps are 1024×1024. Generated normal vectors are
unit length before 8-bit quantization (within approximately 1.1% afterward).
The original source normal map is retained for scanned colonies. The base
material URLs/checksums are in `assets/materials/manifest.json`; the new
tissue-map manifest is separate under `pocillopora_tissue/`. Licensing and
artist credits are in `assets/LICENSES.md` and each asset's provenance files.
