# Reef Rescue AUV — complete live-demo checklist

Updated 8 November 2026. This consolidates the website, simulation, sensing, control, realism and one-minute judging requests. Preserve the current design system; use **Reef Rescue AUV** throughout.

Status: **Working** means implemented in the browser demo and checked at the stated scope. **Partial** means a useful reduced model exists. **To do** means not yet demonstrated. A browser implementation does not establish Isaac parity or field validity.

The **Robot + base** workflow, fresh-camera gates, semantic memory, camera picture-in-picture and model activity logs are implemented. ## P0 — perception-driven restoration and the judged take

- [x] **Distinct reef regions — working as seeded scenario composition.** Two predominantly healthy patches, two predominantly bleached-but-living patches and a mixed central transition share the same region field across irregular specimen placement, materials and mission colonies. Dead/rubble controls remain excluded. These are generated scenario regions, not measured ecological distributions.
- [ ] **Separate spectator and robot optics — implemented at reduced scope; calibration outstanding.** Tool/forward cameras render independently of spectator framing and omit navigation/selection overlays; picture-in-picture and Gemma capture use actual robot views. The cameras currently share the physical scene fog, materials and white/violet lighting; there is no separate sensor-specific attenuation, backscatter/noise or spectral model. Independent camera poses are implemented, not independently calibrated underwater optics. Validate those effects before claiming realistic perception.
- [ ] **Real camera-driven perception — connected implementation; live episode pending.** Gemma receives the actual timestamped robot image and returns bounded observations. Fresh accepted recipient/donor evidence gates Argon planning; recovery requests fresh fragment evidence and gripper-state agreement. Track capture/request IDs, image hash, model, uncertainty and measured latency. Gemma is currently initializing; no full live success is claimed. Registry truth and synthetic observations remain explicitly separate from model estimates.
- [ ] **UV scan → evidence → compatibility decision — partial.** Show aimed excitation/visible pilot light, dwell/exposure and response on the actual target. Compare eligible and ineligible donors. Use known registered species/genet and the simulated marker consistently; UV colour alone must not establish genotype. Label synthetic UV observations. Validate with the intended robot optical view, not the clear spectator view.
- [x] **Persistent semantic observation memory — implemented at bounded scope.** Browser local storage retains up to 128 accepted Gemma colony-health observations with model/source, capture/request identity, simulation frame time and wall-clock expiry (seven days). Exact scene binding rejects wrong-scene imports. UI import/export/clear and limited historical planner context are implemented. Memory is historical context only: restored entries never satisfy fresh-camera safety gates. Storage failure falls back to session-only memory. This is not a complete persistent map of every pose, action and outcome.
- [ ] **Sensor fusion with uncertainty — partial.** Time-align sonar, camera observations, estimated pose/current and prior map state. Expose provenance and stale/missing/conflicting data. The current fusion combines geometric sonar with synthetic optical registry observations and illustrative confidence. It is not a calibrated estimator. Ensure actual model detections can update the map and planner without overriding safety checks.
- [x] **Current-driven grip loss and autonomous recovery — working at reduced scope.** During carrying, a current disturbance must change modeled loads enough to cause grip loss. Detect loss, observe/search for the same fragment, replan, reacquire, inspect, resume and mechanically verify. Retain the same fragment ID and do not create a replacement. Automated tests cover a modeled current wave exceeding the grip threshold, recovery of the same fragment and verified placement; distinguish injected disturbance from its physical consequences.
- [ ] **One continuous 60-second video — to do.** Rehearse and capture the real sequence with visible speed, controller and evidence source. Do not hide a reset, failed inference or manual correction. Use one main recovery event; turtle crossing can be an alternate take. See [the shot-by-shot plan](ONE_MINUTE_DEMO.md).

- [x] **Manual pilot mode — working.** Keyboard and on-screen controls for translation, depth and yaw; gripper opening/closing; input watchdog; pause/blur safety; local collision/wildlife avoidance stays active. Resume autonomy from the current pose without teleporting or losing fragment state.
- [x] **Two route options — working at reduced scope.** Direct transit and a wider elevated clearance route are actual followed waypoints, with visible alternatives and route metrics.
- [ ] **General dynamic path planning — partial.** Recompute paths from fused obstacles, uncertainty, current costs and moving animals. Demonstrate blocked-path rerouting, narrow passages, unreachable goals and safe abort. Current corridor alternatives and local avoidance are not a full optimal planner.
- [x] **Continuous current field — working at reduced scope.** Spatial/depth/time-varying flow affects vehicle and fragment forces. Report current vector, thrust, drag, buoyancy, velocity and clearance. Flow arrows and advected particles use samples of the same physical field; their display scale is labelled. Zero/current/surge controls must produce measurably different motion.
- [ ] **High-fidelity underwater mechanics — to validate in Isaac.** Full 6-DOF dynamics, rotational drag, added mass, calibrated thruster allocation, contact/friction, realistic gripper slip/force, cutting/fracture and attachment mechanics. Current browser dynamics are reduced 3-DOF forces with approximate collision and grasp constraints; they are not “ultra-realistic physics.”
- [x] **Connected gripper geometry — working visually.** Rails, carriages, joints, pads, cutter, cable and tool camera; grasp/contact coordinates match a triangle on the actual coral scan. Test open/close movement, same-fragment transport and release.
- [ ] **Material-specific response, grasp and cutting — partial.** Stony coral skeletons and rocks stay fixed; a bounded millimetre-scale polyp animation responds to current while detached cuttings obey reduced drag. Validate jaw contact, stem position, stroke, cutting quality, load/slip threshold, tissue mechanics and recovery against physical measurements or Isaac contact tests. Visual linkage alone is insufficient.
- [x] **Moving marine life with navigation consequences — working at reduced scope.** Fish use coherent seeded school trajectories/individual movement; crabs follow the bed and a turtle crosses the habitat. Their authoritative positions produce contacts and yielding behavior.
- [ ] **More realistic wildlife behavior — partial.** Validate separation/alignment/cohesion, obstacle/reef avoidance, varied speeds, rest/feeding behavior and believable crab gait. Avoid mechanically looping identical animals. Demonstrate turtle avoidance rather than deliberately striking wildlife.
- [x] **Five setback controls — working.** Dropped fragment, imprecise cut, unstable placement, current surge and sensor dropout. Recovery budgets, withheld success and safe aborts must remain testable.

## Definition of ready for judging

A new viewer can understand the problem, see sensor evidence cause a decision, introduce one real disturbance, watch autonomous recovery of the same fragment and see a verified placement—all within one continuous minute. Evidence labels accurately distinguish live Gemma perception / Argon reasoning / cuOpt optimization, scripted control, synthetic sensors, known registry facts, reduced browser physics and biological outcomes that are not simulated.
