# Reef Rescue AUV — one-minute demonstration

Record **one uninterrupted 60-second screen capture of the running application**. Keep the mission clock, speed multiplier, controller source and event log visible. Camera changes and a clearly labelled speed change are acceptable; do not splice separate episodes, jump the simulation clock, replace the camera feed with a render, or label a scripted decision as Gemini. Captions may be added over the continuous take without covering evidence.

The main story is one eligible transfer interrupted by an operator-injected current wave: the wave changes the simulated water load, the grip fails in the model, and the robot autonomously recovers and places **the same fragment**. This is a demonstration of bounded recovery in a reduced simulator, not a claim of coral healing or real-world robustness.

Before accepting a take, check the actual exported state and events:

- The source label matches reality: `base_station` must have real Gemma evidence, a successful cuOpt solve and a matching Argon decision. `scripted`, explicit partial mode and legacy `gemini` are separate claims. An unavailable provider is never successful inference.
- The injected current event precedes the grip-loss event, and the recorded modeled load/threshold supports the cause. A separately injected drop is not substituted.
- The fragment ID held before the fault equals the ID recovered and finally placed; donor stock is conserved and no replacement fragment silently appears.
- At least one drop and one recovery are recorded; exactly one required placement is mechanically verified; the episode's own `completed` flag is true. These are model outcomes, not biological outcomes.
- The pilot remains autonomous after fault injection; no manual movement/gripper/route intervention supplies the recovery. Spectator camera changes are recorded separately from robot control.
- The 60-second recording has no hidden cuts, reset, clock jump or unlabelled acceleration. If any required check fails, rehearse again or present the failure honestly.
